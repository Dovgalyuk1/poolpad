02
Settlement

The vote is the oracle.

Prediction markets pay the side that was right. Poolpad pays the side that won the vote — the vault market-buys the winning option and the buy is public, stepped and impossible to cancel. The poll result is the only input.

03 · TRADE

Fees fill the vault

Every trade on any option pays a 2% creator tax. 60% of it goes to the vault in ETH, 25% to the poll author, 15% to the protocol. The vault can only do one thing, and only after the close.

vault 1.84 ETH · +0.02 from a $DOGS sell
market says $CATS 61% · poll says $DOGS 54%
04 · CLOSE

The vault buys the winner

At close the engine posts the poll result on-chain. The vault buys the winning coin in 8 equal steps over 60 minutes and sends the tokens to the burn address. Losing options keep trading, they just never get the buy.

VAULT · 60%Held in ETH until the close. Spent entirely on the winning option, tokens burned. If the winner is a tie the vault splits pro-rata between the tied options.
AUTHOR · 25%Paid to the wallet linked to the account that posted the poll, hourly, from every option. You do not need to pick the winner to be paid.
PROTOCOL · 15%Two thirds buy and burn $POOL on the open market. One third funds the engine, the result posting and the RPC bill.

What stops vote buying?

Nothing stops it, and it is the point — a vote is worth exactly what the vault will spend on it. But the buy is a 60-minute TWAP, capped per step at 12% of the option's curve liquidity, so a bought vote does not turn into a one-block spike.

What if nobody votes?

Polls that close with fewer than 100 votes settle pro-rata: the vault buys every option in proportion to its vote share. A poll with zero votes buys every option equally. The vault never stays unspent.

Can the author cancel?

No. The close block is written into every option coin at launch and the vault has no owner, no pause and no upgrade. If the post is deleted the poll still closes on the last snapshot the engine took.
